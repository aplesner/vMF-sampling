Gate G1 — restated and verified

v1 text (unsatisfiable): “mean learned κ falls monotonically with d while KL still rises.” KL at fixed κ is non-monotone in m, peaking at m* ≈ 0.762κ; the conjunction is only satisfiable on the rising branch, κ/m ≳ 1.31. On the extrapolated Davidson trend that boundary is crossed between d = 64 and d = 128, so at d ≥ 256 the v1 gate fails regardless of the science.

Restated gate (full text in PREREGISTRATION.md): G1a ρ̄(d) = mean_x A_m(κ(x)) non-increasing in d; G1b KL/m ≥ r₀ = 0.02 nats/dim at every d (rules out the wall being mere collapse to the uniform prior; f(ρ) = KL/m is strictly increasing, so G1b ⇔ ρ̄ ≳ 0.2); G1c (report, not pass/fail) sign of ρ̄ − ρ* per d, with ρ* = 0.69. G1 passes iff G1a ∧ G1b. The conjunction is jointly satisfiable on both branches of KL(m).

V1 & V5 — the restated quantities are sound and computable

KL = m·f(ρ) at fixed ρ: relative spread of KL/m across m = 8…4096 is ≤ 1e-3 for ρ ≤ 0.3 (anchor: ρ=0.3 gives 0.04715 at m = 128, 1024, 4096 — the brief’s value), ≤ 2.5e-2 at ρ = 0.5 and converging in m. At the trend operating point, scipy’s ive(order, κ̂) is exactly 0 from m = 1024 upward, while the CUSF/torch log-Bessel path keeps every gate quantity finite through m = 8192. KL evaluation switches to a first-order asymptotic form at κ* = 8.2e3·m3/4 (worst jump at the switch 2.6e-3 nats); round-trip inversion tests are exact. Full log: results/g1_verification_log.txt; CSVs under results/.

T1a — the reference S-VAE is numerically invalid at high m

Reproducing the exact numerics of Davidson’s reference implementation (KL via scipy.special.ive, eq.-6 ive-ratio gradient): the forward KL and its gradient go non-finite at the κ values an S-VAE encoder actually emits (softplus init ≈ 0.54, trend κ ≈ 3–20) from m = 512 upward, and in 9/10 probed (κ, m) cells at m ≥ 1024 — the loss is NaN before training starts. Our CUSF/torch log-Bessel path agrees with the reference to 3.7e-11 (max rel) wherever the reference is finite, and stays finite through m = 4096 (tested through 8192 in the unit tests). Full grid: results/t1_numerical_validity.csv.

Two-GPU protocol (T2/T3)

Two RTX 3090s as independent workers — one (arm × d × seed) run per card, never DDP. Every arm gets its own LR sweep; no arm is compared at a shared LR. Card assignment is recorded per run so CRN pairing uses seed, not card.
